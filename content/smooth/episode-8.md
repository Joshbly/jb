preview: A dental office in Sherman Oaks. The chairs are mauve. The fish in the corner tank are doing their best. The wait is long.

*FADE IN:*

*A dental office. Sherman Oaks. Tuesday. 10:15 AM.*

*The waiting room is beige. The chairs are mauve. A fish tank bubbles in the corner. The fish are not interesting. They're just fish. They're doing their best.*

*On the wall: a framed photograph of a boat. Beneath the boat, in cursive: "Smooth Sailing."*

*Seated in a row, like men awaiting sentencing:*

*MICHAEL McDONALD.*

*KENNY LOGGINS.*

*CHRISTOPHER CROSS.*

*They are not speaking.*

*They have not spoken since the parking lot.*

*Some silences are comfortable.*

*This one has a job to do.*

---

*A RECEPTIONIST slides open the frosted glass window.*

---

**RECEPTIONIST:**

Mr. McDonald? Dr. Kellerman will see you in about ten minutes. He's just finishing up.

**McDONALD:**

Thank you.

*The window slides closed.*

---

**LOGGINS:**

*(quietly)*

I don't like the new hygienist.

**McDONALD:**

There's a new hygienist?

**LOGGINS:**

Started in March. She doesn't know the protocol.

**McDONALD:**

What protocol?

**LOGGINS:**

The music protocol, Michael. Last time I was here, she put on *Enya*.

**McDONALD:**

During the cleaning?

**LOGGINS:**

During the *rinse*.

---

*CROSS looks up from a magazine. The magazine is "Yachting World." It is from 2019. He has read it eleven times.*

---

**CROSS:**

Enya is not smooth.

**LOGGINS:**

I know.

**CROSS:**

Enya is... atmospheric.

**LOGGINS:**

I know.

**CROSS:**

Enya is what plays in a spa when they're trying to convince you that cucumbers on your eyes are a spiritual experience.

**McDONALD:**

Christopher.

**CROSS:**

I'm just saying.

---

*The door to the waiting room opens.*

*A man enters.*

*He is tall. His hair is silver but full—defiantly full, the hair of a man who has never once thought about hair loss and therefore has been spared it. His shirt is silk. His teeth, even from across the room, are visible. They are very white. Aggressively white. The white of a man who has been to many dentists and has defeated all of them.*

*It is BARRY GIBB.*

---

*He approaches the reception window.*

*He does not see the yacht rock boys.*

*Or maybe he sees them and does not recognize them.*

*It is unclear which is worse.*

---

**BARRY:**

*(to the receptionist, through the glass)*

Barry Gibb. Ten-thirty.

**RECEPTIONIST:**

Of course, Mr. Gibb. Please have a seat. Dr. Kellerman will be right with you.

---

*Barry turns.*

*He sees the chairs.*

*He sees the men in the chairs.*

*He smiles.*

*The smile is warm. Genuine. The smile of a man who has no enemies because he has never understood that enemies are possible.*

---

**BARRY:**

Oh, hello.

---

*No one responds.*

---

**BARRY:**

Mind if I sit?

---

*He gestures at the empty chair next to Cross.*

*Cross looks at the chair.*

*He looks at Barry.*

*He looks at the chair again.*

---

**CROSS:**

It's a free country.

**BARRY:**

That it is.

---

*He sits.*

*The cushion sighs beneath him.*

*Everything sighs around Barry Gibb. Cushions. Women. The very air.*

---

**BARRY:**

Bit of a wait, they said?

**McDONALD:**

Ten minutes.

**BARRY:**

Not bad. Not bad at all. I've been in waiting rooms for hours. Back in the day. Before the teeth got sorted.

*He taps his own mouth.*

**BARRY:**

Veneers. Eighty-six. Best decision I ever made. Well. Second best. First best was the harmonies.

---

*Silence.*

---

**BARRY:**

You fellows musicians?

**McDONALD:**

...yes.

**BARRY:**

Thought so. I can always tell. Something about the hands.

*He gestures vaguely at their hands.*

**BARRY:**

Musician hands. My brothers had them. I've got them. You've got them.

---

*LOGGINS looks at his own hands.*

*He has never thought about his hands.*

*Now he will think about them forever.*

---

**BARRY:**

What sort of music?

**McDONALD:**

Soft rock. Mostly.

**BARRY:**

Oh, lovely. Lovely stuff, soft rock. Very soothing. Very—what's the word—

**McDONALD:**

Smooth.

**BARRY:**

That's it. Smooth.

---

*The word hangs in the air.*

*It does not fall.*

*It just hangs there.*

*Hovering.*

*Waiting to see what happens next.*

---

*The inner door opens.*

*DR. KELLERMAN emerges.*

*He is sixty-one. He has a mustache that suggests he was once more interesting than he is now. He wears a white coat. On the pocket of the coat: an embroidered anchor.*

*He sees Barry.*

*He sees the yacht rock boys.*

*His face lights up.*

---

**DR. KELLERMAN:**

Well, well, well. Look at this. My whole smooth rock collection in one room.

---

*The room temperature drops.*

*Imperceptibly.*

*But it drops.*

---

**DR. KELLERMAN:**

Mr. Gibb, welcome. I see you've met some colleagues.

**BARRY:**

We were just chatting. Lovely fellows.

**DR. KELLERMAN:**

The loveliest. Michael here has been coming to me for—what is it now, Michael? Fifteen years?

**McDONALD:**

Sixteen.

**DR. KELLERMAN:**

Sixteen years. Never a cavity. Cleanest teeth in soft rock. Present company included.

*He winks at Barry.*

*Barry winks back.*

*They are winking at each other.*

*This is happening.*

---

**DR. KELLERMAN:**

You know, I was just telling my hygienist the other day—I've got the whole smooth crowd. I've got the McDonald. I've got the Loggins.

**LOGGINS:**

*(weakly)*

Hi.

**DR. KELLERMAN:**

I've got the Cross—

**CROSS:**

*(not looking up)*

Hello.

**DR. KELLERMAN:**

And now I've got a Bee Gee. All the smooth guys. One practice.

---

*He says it so casually.*

*"All the smooth guys."*

*Like it's nothing.*

*Like the categories don't matter.*

*Like decades of aesthetic refinement can be collapsed into a single waiting room by a man with a water pick.*

---

**McDONALD:**

Dr. Kellerman.

**DR. KELLERMAN:**

Yes, Michael?

**McDONALD:**

Could I speak with you for a moment?

**DR. KELLERMAN:**

Of course. Everything alright? Sensitivity?

**McDONALD:**

No. I just... I have a question. About the practice.

**DR. KELLERMAN:**

Sure thing. Step into my office.

---

*McDonald stands.*

*He follows Kellerman through the inner door.*

*The door closes.*

---

*Silence.*

---

**BARRY:**

Seems like a nice fellow.

**LOGGINS:**

He is.

**BARRY:**

Good dentist?

**LOGGINS:**

The best.

**BARRY:**

Gentle?

**LOGGINS:**

Very gentle.

**BARRY:**

That's what I look for. Gentleness. Can't stand the rough ones. The scrapers. You know the type.

**LOGGINS:**

I do.

**BARRY:**

My brother Maurice—God rest him—he had a dentist in Miami, absolute butcher. Maurice would come out of there looking like he'd been in a bar fight. I said to him, I said, "Maurice, there are gentle dentists out there. You just have to find them."

**LOGGINS:**

And did he?

**BARRY:**

He did. Eventually. Dr. Ruiz. Lovely man. Cuban. Very soft hands.

---

*CROSS sets down his magazine.*

---

**CROSS:**

Mr. Gibb.

**BARRY:**

Please. Barry.

**CROSS:**

Barry.

**BARRY:**

Yes?

**CROSS:**

Can I ask you something?

**BARRY:**

Of course.

**CROSS:**

Do you consider yourself smooth?

---

*Barry tilts his head.*

*Like a golden retriever that has just been asked to define "justice."*

---

**BARRY:**

Smooth?

**CROSS:**

Yes.

**BARRY:**

In what sense?

**CROSS:**

In the... categorical sense.

**BARRY:**

I'm not sure I follow.

**CROSS:**

Dr. Kellerman called us "the smooth guys." He grouped us together. Do you think that's accurate?

**BARRY:**

Well, I suppose—I mean, we all make soft music, don't we? Music that's easy on the ears. Music for relaxation. Music for—

**CROSS:**

Dentist offices.

**BARRY:**

*(laughing)*

Yes! Exactly. Dentist offices. Waiting rooms. Lifts. The backgrounds of things.

---

*Cross leans forward.*

---

**CROSS:**

But is that smooth?

**BARRY:**

Isn't it?

**CROSS:**

No. That's *ambient*. That's *inoffensive*. That's *present but ignorable*. Smooth is something else entirely.

**BARRY:**

What is it then?

---

*Cross opens his mouth.*

*Nothing comes out.*

*He closes it.*

*He opens it again.*

---

**CROSS:**

I don't know how to explain it.

**BARRY:**

Try.

**CROSS:**

Smooth is... it's a feeling. A specific feeling. It's sunset on water. It's the moment before something happens, but nothing needs to happen, because the moment is enough. It's resolution without tension. It's arrival without departure.

**BARRY:**

That sounds lovely.

**CROSS:**

It IS lovely. It's the loveliest thing there is. And it's *ours*.

**BARRY:**

Whose?

**CROSS:**

Yacht rock. The genre. The community. The—the *people*.

**BARRY:**

And I'm not part of that?

---

*Silence.*

---

**LOGGINS:**

*(quietly)*

It's not personal, Barry.

**BARRY:**

It feels a bit personal.

**LOGGINS:**

It's just... you're disco.

---

*The word lands.*

*Barry blinks.*

---

**BARRY:**

I'm not disco.

**LOGGINS:**

"Stayin' Alive"?

**BARRY:**

That's one song.

**LOGGINS:**

"Night Fever"?

**BARRY:**

That's two songs.

**LOGGINS:**

The entire Saturday Night Fever soundtrack?

**BARRY:**

That was a *moment*, Kenny. A cultural moment. We didn't invent disco. We just... participated.

**LOGGINS:**

You DEFINED it, Barry. You were the face of it. You were on the poster. Literally on the poster. John Travolta's finger pointing up and YOUR VOICE coming out of the speakers.

**BARRY:**

And that disqualifies me from being smooth?

**LOGGINS:**

Disco is urgent. Disco is 120 BPM. Disco is dancing. Smooth is NOT dancing. Smooth is the opposite of dancing. Smooth is sitting very still on a boat and letting the music wash over you like weather.

**BARRY:**

I've been on boats.

**LOGGINS:**

EVERYONE'S been on boats, Barry. Being on a boat doesn't make you yacht rock.

---

**BARRY:**

Then what does?

---

*The inner door opens.*

*McDonald emerges.*

*His face is unreadable.*

---

**McDONALD:**

Kenny. Christopher. Can I talk to you?

**LOGGINS:**

Now?

**McDONALD:**

Now.

---

*They stand.*

*They follow McDonald to the corner of the waiting room.*

*They huddle.*

*Barry watches from his chair.*

*The fish in the tank watch from their tank.*

*Everyone is watching everyone.*

---

**McDONALD:**

*(hushed)*

Kellerman won't budge.

**LOGGINS:**

What do you mean?

**McDONALD:**

I tried to explain. I told him the Bee Gees aren't smooth. I told him there's a difference. A categorical difference. I used the words "ontological distinction."

**CROSS:**

And?

**McDONALD:**

He said, "Michael, I just clean teeth."

**LOGGINS:**

That's it?

**McDONALD:**

He said, "To me, you're all just the soft rock station." He said, "I can't tell the difference between 'What a Fool Believes' and 'How Deep Is Your Love.'"

---

*Cross makes a sound.*

*It's not a word.*

*It's something older than words.*

*A grunt of primal devastation.*

---

**CROSS:**

He can't tell the difference?

**McDONALD:**

He said they both "have that nice flow."

**CROSS:**

"That nice flow."

**McDONALD:**

I tried, Christopher. I tried to explain what smooth means. What it *really* means. The philosophy. The aesthetic. The boats.

**LOGGINS:**

And?

**McDONALD:**

He showed me a picture of his boat.

**LOGGINS:**

What's it called?

**McDONALD:**

"Molar Eclipse."

---

*Silence.*

---

**LOGGINS:**

A pun.

**McDONALD:**

A dental pun.

**CROSS:**

This man was supposed to be our ally.

**McDONALD:**

He was never our ally, Christopher. He was a fan. And fans don't understand. Fans just *consume*. They put us on in the background while they scrape plaque and they think that makes them part of it. But they're not part of it. They're just... audience.

---

*Barry clears his throat.*

*They turn.*

*He's standing now.*

*He has approached the huddle.*

*The huddle has been breached.*

---

**BARRY:**

I couldn't help but overhear.

**McDONALD:**

Barry—

**BARRY:**

No, no. It's alright. I understand now. You have a community. A... a club. And I'm not in it.

**LOGGINS:**

It's not a club.

**BARRY:**

It sounds like a club.

**McDONALD:**

It's a *genre*, Barry.

**BARRY:**

Genres are just clubs with better marketing.

---

*Silence.*

*That's... actually a fair point.*

*No one says this out loud.*

---

**BARRY:**

You know, my brothers and I—we went through something similar. After disco died. After the backlash. People burned our records, Michael. Literally burned them. In a stadium. They called it "Disco Demolition Night." They put our faces on the posters and they set fire to everything we'd made.

**McDONALD:**

I know.

**BARRY:**

Do you? Do you know what that's like? To have people decide you're not just *bad* but *dangerous*? To have a genre become a slur?

**McDONALD:**

...no.

**BARRY:**

We survived it. We kept making music. Different music. Softer music. Music that didn't make people want to set things on fire. We wrote for other people. We stayed alive.

*He pauses.*

**BARRY:**

That's a joke. "Stayin' Alive." We stayed alive.

**LOGGINS:**

I got it.

**BARRY:**

Good. I wasn't sure it landed.

---

*He looks at them. One by one.*

*McDonald.*

*Loggins.*

*Cross.*

---

**BARRY:**

I don't need to be in your club, gentlemen. I've had clubs. I've been in clubs and out of clubs and I've watched clubs burn to the ground while people cheered. I just came here to get my teeth cleaned.

---

*He turns toward the inner door.*

*Dr. Kellerman is standing there.*

---

**DR. KELLERMAN:**

Mr. Gibb? Ready for you.

**BARRY:**

Lovely.

---

*He starts to walk.*

*He stops.*

*He turns back.*

---

**BARRY:**

One more thing.

**McDONALD:**

Yes?

**BARRY:**

"How Deep Is Your Love" came out in 1977. "What a Fool Believes" came out in 1979. If anyone influenced anyone...

*He lets it hang.*

**BARRY:**

...well.

---

*He walks through the door.*

*The door closes.*

---

*Silence.*

---

**LOGGINS:**

Did he just—

**McDONALD:**

Don't.

**LOGGINS:**

He implied that we—

**McDONALD:**

I said don't.

**CROSS:**

1977.

**McDONALD:**

Christopher.

**CROSS:**

"How Deep Is Your Love" was 1977.

**McDONALD:**

I KNOW WHAT YEAR IT WAS.

**CROSS:**

"Sailing" was 1980.

---

*McDonald turns.*

---

**McDONALD:**

What are you saying?

**CROSS:**

I'm not saying anything.

**McDONALD:**

It sounds like you're saying something.

**CROSS:**

I'm just... noting. Dates. Chronology.

**LOGGINS:**

Christopher, you can't seriously be suggesting—

**CROSS:**

I'm not suggesting anything.

---

*But he is.*

*They all know he is.*

*The suggestion is in the room now.*

*It cannot be un-suggested.*

---

*What if the Bee Gees came first?*

*What if "How Deep Is Your Love"—with its soft harmonies, its gentle pulse, its question that isn't really a question—was the template?*

*What if yacht rock didn't invent smooth?*

*What if yacht rock just... refined it?*

*What if the Bee Gees were the source?*

*What if—*

---

**McDONALD:**

No.

**LOGGINS:**

Michael—

**McDONALD:**

NO. I refuse. "How Deep Is Your Love" is a love song. A DISCO love song. From a DISCO MOVIE. It's played in roller rinks. It's played at proms. It's played in contexts where people are MOVING. People MOVE to that song, Kenny.

**LOGGINS:**

People move to our songs too.

**McDONALD:**

People SWAY to our songs. SWAYING AND MOVING ARE DIFFERENT.

**CROSS:**

Are they though?

**McDONALD:**

CHRISTOPHER, I SWEAR TO GOD—

---

*The receptionist slides open the window.*

---

**RECEPTIONIST:**

Gentlemen? Is everything okay out here?

**McDONALD:**

*(composing himself)*

Yes. Yes. Everything's fine.

**RECEPTIONIST:**

Mr. McDonald, Dr. Kellerman is ready for your cleaning now.

**McDONALD:**

Thank you.

---

*He straightens his shirt.*

*He walks toward the inner door.*

*He stops.*

---

**McDONALD:**

This conversation isn't over.

**LOGGINS:**

I know.

**McDONALD:**

We need to... we need to do research. We need to establish a timeline. We need to figure out, definitively, once and for all, who invented smooth.

**CROSS:**

And if it was them?

---

*McDonald doesn't answer.*

*He walks through the door.*

*The door closes.*

---

*Loggins and Cross sit back down.*

*The fish tank bubbles.*

*The photograph of the boat watches them.*

*"Smooth Sailing."*

*But what does that even mean?*

*What does any of it mean?*

---

**LOGGINS:**

Kenny?

**CROSS:**

Yeah?

**LOGGINS:**

What if we're not the originals?

**CROSS:**

Don't.

**LOGGINS:**

What if we're just... a subgenre? A refinement? A *response*?

**CROSS:**

I said don't.

**LOGGINS:**

What if the Bee Gees invented smooth and we just—

**CROSS:**

KENNY.

---

*Loggins stops.*

---

**CROSS:**

Some questions don't need answers. Some questions are better left in the waiting room.

---

*He picks up his magazine.*

*"Yachting World."*

*2019.*

*He's read it eleven times.*

*Now he'll read it again.*

*It's the only thing that makes sense anymore.*

---

*FADE TO BLACK.*

---

*"Barry Gibb sent a card."*

---

*"The question of who invented smooth remains unresolved."*

*"It will remain unresolved."*

*"Some things are smoother that way."*

**END OF EPISODE.**

---

*POST-CREDITS SCENE:*

*A recording studio. Miami. 1977.*

*BARRY, ROBIN, and MAURICE GIBB stand around a microphone.*

*They are about to record "How Deep Is Your Love."*

*Barry plays the opening chord.*

*The room fills with something.*

*Not light.*

*Something else.*

*Something that doesn't have a name yet.*

---

**BARRY:**

Do you feel that?

**ROBIN:**

Feel what?

**BARRY:**

That... smoothness.

**MAURICE:**

Is that what we're calling it?

**BARRY:**

I don't know what else to call it.

---

*They look at each other.*

*They don't know what they're starting.*

*No one ever does.*

---

**ROBIN:**

Let's just record the song.

**BARRY:**

Yeah. Let's just record the song.

---

*He plays the chord again.*

*And somewhere, in a future that hasn't happened yet, in a waiting room that doesn't exist, in a genre that has no name—*

*—something begins.*

*"In a room in San Antonio."*

*"Alone."*

*"Neither of them knows this."*

*"Neither of them will ever know."*
