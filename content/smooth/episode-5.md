---
number: 5
title: The Southern Question
preview: A dinner party at Michael McDonald's Malibu house. The ocean is the only guest that never leaves. A question is about to be asked.
---

*FADE IN:*

*A dinner party. MICHAEL McDONALD's house. Malibu. The house is exactly what you think it is: white walls, blond wood, a kitchen island the size of a compact car. Through the windows: the ocean. Always the ocean. The ocean is the only guest that never leaves.*

*Seated around a long table:*

*MICHAEL McDONALD at the head.*

*KENNY LOGGINS to his left, recently re-admitted, on good behavior.*

*CHRISTOPHER CROSS to his right, silent, holding a wine glass he has not sipped from in forty minutes.*

*STEVE LUKATHER and DAVID PAICH, representing Toto, sharing a chair because they arrived together and McDonald only has seven chairs.*

*DARYL HALL. Alone. JOHN OATES was invited but did not come. No one has addressed this.*

*And at the far end of the table: ROBBIE DUPREE.*

*No one remembers inviting Robbie Dupree.*

*No one ever remembers inviting Robbie Dupree.*

*He is always just there.*

---

*They are eating salmon. The salmon is poached. Poaching is the smoothest method of cooking. Grilling is for rock. Frying is for country. Poaching is for men who understand that heat should be *implied* rather than *applied.* *

---

**McDONALD:**

I'd like to thank everyone for coming.

**LOGGINS:**

Of course, Mike.

**McDONALD:**

It's been a difficult year.

**HALL:**

The streaming residuals.

**McDONALD:**

The streaming residuals, yes.

**LUKATHER:**

And the podcast.

---

*Everyone stiffens.*

---

**McDONALD:**

We don't talk about the podcast.

**LUKATHER:**

Sorry.

**McDONALD:**

A man starts a podcast *one time* about the yacht rock community and suddenly everyone's an expert. Suddenly everyone has *opinions.* I had a twenty-three-year-old at a coffee shop tell me that "I Keep Forgettin'" was "mid." *Mid,* Kenny.

**LOGGINS:**

What does that mean?

**McDONALD:**

I don't know. I went home and cried.

**CROSS:**

*(not looking up)*

You cried in key though.

**McDONALD:**

I always cry in key.

---

*ROBBIE DUPREE clears his throat.*

*Everyone turns.*

*They had forgotten he was there.*

*This happens often.*

---

**DUPREE:**

I have something to say.

**McDONALD:**

Robbie, we're in the middle of—

**DUPREE:**

I need to say it.

**HALL:**

Can it wait until dessert?

**DUPREE:**

It's waited forty years.

---

*Silence.*

*The salmon steams.*

*Somewhere, a clock ticks, but the clock is in another room, so the ticking is very faint, almost a memory of ticking rather than ticking itself.*

---

**DUPREE:**

I want to talk about "Southern Cross."

---

*McDONALD drops his fork.*

*LOGGINS chokes on nothing. He was not eating. He choked on air. On the *idea.* *

*HALL stands up so fast his chair falls backward.*

*LUKATHER and PAICH, sharing their chair, both try to stand at the same time and the chair tips over and they fall in a pile but no one helps them because everyone is frozen.*

*CROSS does not move. CROSS never moves. But his grip on the wine glass tightens. A crack appears in the stem.*

---

**McDONALD:**

Robbie.

**DUPREE:**

Michael.

**McDONALD:**

We don't—

**DUPREE:**

I know we don't. That's why I am.

---

**HALL:**

*(from the floor, retrieving his chair)*

This is wildly inappropriate.

**DUPREE:**

Is it, Daryl? Is it inappropriate? Or is it *overdue?*

**HALL:**

It's inappropriate *because* it's overdue. Some things are overdue for a reason. Some things stay in the past because the past is where they *belong.*

**DUPREE:**

It's a SONG, Daryl. It's a song about a MAN on a BOAT using STARS to NAVIGATE while processing EMOTIONAL PAIN. That is *literally* the mission statement of yacht rock.

**HALL:**

You're oversimplifying.

**DUPREE:**

The chorus is "THINK ABOUT HOW MANY TIMES I HAVE FALLEN." It's a HARMONY. A THREE-PART HARMONY. About FALLING and FINDING DIRECTION. On a BOAT.

---

*McDONALD holds up his hand.*

---

**McDONALD:**

Robbie, I'm going to ask you to lower your voice.

**DUPREE:**

My voice is at a normal volume.

**McDONALD:**

Your voice is at a *confrontational* volume. Yacht rock doesn't do confrontational. Yacht rock does *conversational.*

**DUPREE:**

Fine.

*(quieter)*

Why don't we talk about "Southern Cross?"

---

*Long pause.*

---

**McDONALD:**

Because.

**DUPREE:**

Because why?

**McDONALD:**

Because... it's complicated.

**DUPREE:**

*Complicated?* Mike, I sang "Steal Away." That song is literally about sneaking off to have sex. THAT'S complicated. "Southern Cross" is a man on a boat. It's the least complicated thing there is.

**McDONALD:**

It's not about the song, Robbie.

**DUPREE:**

Then what's it about?

---

*McDONALD looks at the table.*

*He looks at his salmon.*

*He looks at the ocean through the window, eternal and indifferent, smooth as always, smooth as it was before any of them were born and smooth as it will be after all of them are gone.*

---

**McDONALD:**

It's about Crosby.

---

*The name lands like a brick in a baptismal font.*

---

**LOGGINS:**

Jesus.

**HALL:**

Michael.

**McDONALD:**

I said it. I'm not taking it back.

---

**DUPREE:**

What *about* Crosby?

**McDONALD:**

Robbie, do you know what David Crosby did in 1982?

**DUPREE:**

He released—

**McDONALD:**

He got arrested, Robbie. He got arrested with a .45 caliber pistol and freebase cocaine in a nightclub in Dallas. A *nightclub.* Not a marina. Not a yacht club. A nightclub called Cardi's. Do you know what happens at a place called Cardi's?

**DUPREE:**

I—

**McDONALD:**

NEITHER DO I, ROBBIE. AND I DON'T WANT TO KNOW.

---

**CROSS:**

*(quietly, still holding the cracked wine glass)*

He also had a knife.

**McDONALD:**

Thank you, Christopher. He also had a knife.

**CROSS:**

And Quaaludes.

**McDONALD:**

And Quaaludes.

**CROSS:**

And he was running from a different arrest.

**McDONALD:**

AND HE WAS RUNNING FROM A DIFFERENT ARREST.

---

*DUPREE sits back.*

---

**DUPREE:**

So... we don't acknowledge "Southern Cross" because David Crosby did drugs?

**McDONALD:**

Robbie, we ALL did drugs. This is the music industry. I've done things I'm not proud of. We've all done things. But there's a *spectrum.* There's a spectrum from "recreational cocaine at a listening party" to "fleeing arrest with a loaded firearm in the state of Texas." And David Crosby is not on the spectrum. David Crosby is in a *different building* from the spectrum. David Crosby is in a building that's on fire and he's *fanning the flames.*

---

**LUKATHER:**

*(from the floor, still tangled with Paich)*

He also had a lot of children.

**McDONALD:**

What?

**LUKATHER:**

The sperm donations. He fathered a lot of children. Melissa Etheridge's kids. Others. Many others.

**PAICH:**

How many?

**LUKATHER:**

No one knows. *He* doesn't know. He just... distributed himself. Across the population.

**McDONALD:**

This isn't relevant, Steve.

**LUKATHER:**

I think it's relevant. You can't be yacht rock and also be... *omnipresent.* Yacht rock is specific. Yacht rock is contained. David Crosby is *everywhere.* There are children who don't know they're David Crosby's children. They're walking around. Living lives. They could be *anyone.*

**PAICH:**

They could be in this room.

---

*Everyone looks at each other.*

---

**LOGGINS:**

I'm not David Crosby's child.

**HALL:**

No one said you were.

**LOGGINS:**

I just wanted to clarify.

**McDONALD:**

We didn't need clarification, Kenny.

**LOGGINS:**

My father was Gerald Loggins of Everett, Washington. He sold insurance. He had a mustache, but not *that* mustache.

**McDONALD:**

KENNY.

**LOGGINS:**

I just want it on the record.

---

*DUPREE stands.*

---

**DUPREE:**

This is insane.

**McDONALD:**

Robbie—

**DUPREE:**

No, Michael. This is INSANE. You've excluded a perfect yacht rock song—maybe THE most yacht rock song ever written—because one of the singers had a chaotic personal life? Michael, you sang on a DOOBIE BROTHERS album. Do you know what "doobie" means?

**McDONALD:**

That's different.

**DUPREE:**

HOW?

**McDONALD:**

Because we were smooth about it, Robbie. We were *smooth.* We did our substances and we showed up and we sang our harmonies and we went home. We didn't bring WEAPONS. We didn't have CONFRONTATIONS. We didn't make it WEIRD.

**DUPREE:**

CROSBY, STILLS AND NASH PLAYED WOODSTOCK, MICHAEL.

---

*Gasps.*

*Actual gasps.*

*CROSS drops his wine glass. It shatters. No one cleans it up. The wine spreads across the floor like blood from a wound that will never heal.*

---

**McDONALD:**

That's exactly it.

**DUPREE:**

What?

**McDONALD:**

WOODSTOCK, Robbie. They played WOODSTOCK. Do you understand what that means? Do you understand what that *disqualifies* them from?

**DUPREE:**

I—

**McDONALD:**

Woodstock is the opposite of yacht rock. Woodstock is mud. Woodstock is crowds. Woodstock is Jimi Hendrix playing the national anthem through a wall of feedback while people cry and hug and do LSD in a field. Yacht rock is CLEAN, Robbie. Yacht rock is PRIVATE. Yacht rock is a man alone on a boat with a glass of white wine and his thoughts. You can't play Woodstock and be yacht rock. It's ontologically impossible.

---

**DUPREE:**

"Southern Cross" came out thirteen years after Woodstock.

**McDONALD:**

Woodstock doesn't have a statute of limitations.

**DUPREE:**

It should.

**McDONALD:**

Well, it doesn't.

---

*HALL steps forward.*

---

**HALL:**

There's also the Neil Young problem.

**DUPREE:**

Oh, come on.

**HALL:**

It has to be said. Crosby, Stills and Nash are inextricable from Crosby, Stills, Nash and Young. And Neil Young is—

**EVERYONE:**

*(in unison, automatically, like a reflex)*

—not smooth.

**HALL:**

Neil Young is the least smooth man in the history of recorded music. Neil Young is feedback given human form. Neil Young wears flannel *on purpose.* Neil Young made an album called *Trans* where he used a vocoder for the entire thing.

**LOGGINS:**

A vocoder?

**HALL:**

A vocoder, Kenny.

**LOGGINS:**

That's...

**HALL:**

I know.

---

**DUPREE:**

But Neil Young isn't ON "Southern Cross."

**HALL:**

Doesn't matter.

**DUPREE:**

How does that not matter?

**HALL:**

Because once you've been in a band with Neil Young, the Neil Young is *in you.* It's in your blood. Your cells. Your harmonies. Every note Stephen Stills has sung since 1969 has Neil Young's chaos embedded in it at a molecular level.

**DUPREE:**

That's not how music works.

**HALL:**

That's exactly how music works, Robbie. Why do you think we have these dinners? Why do you think we have the marina meetings? Why do you think there's a *certification process?* Because smoothness is FRAGILE. It can be CONTAMINATED. One wrong influence—one chaotic collaborator—and the smooth is GONE.

---

*DUPREE looks around the room.*

*Everyone is nodding.*

*Even CROSS, who never nods, is nodding, almost imperceptibly, like a buoy in gentle water.*

---

**DUPREE:**

So that's it? "Southern Cross"—a song about celestial navigation, about using the stars to find your way home, about healing from loss through the act of sailing—is excluded forever because David Crosby once had a gun and Stephen Stills was in a band with Neil Young?

**McDONALD:**

When you put it that way, it sounds unreasonable.

**DUPREE:**

IT IS UNREASONABLE.

**McDONALD:**

But it's also correct.

---

*Silence.*

*A long silence.*

*The kind of silence that has its own weather system.*

---

*And then:*

*The doorbell rings.*

---

*Everyone freezes.*

---

**McDONALD:**

I'm not expecting anyone.

**LOGGINS:**

Should I...?

**McDONALD:**

No. I'll get it.

---

*He walks to the door.*

*He opens it.*

*Standing there, backlit by the Malibu sunset, wearing a suede vest with no shirt underneath, his mustache enormous and slightly damp, a guitar case in one hand and a bottle of something unlabeled in the other, is:*

**DAVID CROSBY.**

---

**CROSBY:**

I heard you were talking about me.

---

*McDONALD does not move.*

*Behind him, someone—maybe Loggins, maybe Paich, it's impossible to tell—makes a small sound like a balloon losing air very slowly.*

---

**McDONALD:**

David.

**CROSBY:**

Michael.

**McDONALD:**

How did you—

**CROSBY:**

I always know, Michael. When someone talks about "Southern Cross," I feel it. In my liver. My replacement liver. The one I got in '94. It's tuned to the frequency of the song. Every time someone even *thinks* about that track, it vibrates. Like a tuning fork. Like a *homing beacon.*

---

*He pushes past McDonald.*

*He enters the dining room.*

*Everyone is standing now. Even Lukather and Paich, who have finally untangled themselves.*

---

**CROSBY:**

Well, well, well. The smooth boys. The *yacht* boys. Having your little dinner. Eating your little salmon. Talking about who's in and who's out.

**McDONALD:**

David, this is a private—

**CROSBY:**

NOTHING'S PRIVATE, MICHAEL.

*He sets the guitar case on the table. On top of the salmon. No one stops him.*

**CROSBY:**

You know what I was doing in 1982? When I wrote my parts on "Southern Cross"?

**HALL:**

Getting arrested?

**CROSBY:**

BESIDES getting arrested. I was LIVING, Daryl. I was EXPERIENCING. I was consuming REALITY in all its forms—chemical, emotional, legal, illegal—and I was CHANNELING that into HARMONY. Do you know what harmony is?

**HALL:**

I—

**CROSBY:**

Harmony is CONFLICT RESOLVED. Harmony is taking two notes that shouldn't work and making them work ANYWAY. That's what I do. That's what I've ALWAYS done. I am CONFLICT, and I am RESOLUTION, and I am BOTH AT THE SAME TIME.

---

*He opens the guitar case.*

*Inside is not a guitar.*

*Inside is another, smaller guitar case.*

*He opens that one.*

*Inside THAT is a guitar.*

---

**CROSBY:**

I'm going to play it.

**McDONALD:**

David—

**CROSBY:**

I'M GOING TO PLAY "SOUTHERN CROSS" RIGHT NOW IN YOUR DINING ROOM AND YOU'RE ALL GOING TO LISTEN AND THEN YOU'RE GOING TO TELL ME IT'S NOT SMOOTH.

**McDONALD:**

Please don't.

**CROSBY:**

TOO LATE.

---

*He strums.*

*The opening chord fills the room.*

*It is... smooth.*

*Undeniably smooth.*

*The smoothness is almost aggressive. It's smooth the way a glacier is smooth—ancient, unstoppable, indifferent to human concerns.*

---

*CROSBY begins to sing.*

---

**CROSBY:**
*(singing)*

Got out of town on a boat goin' to Southern islands...

---

*Something happens to the room.*

*The lights seem to dim. The ocean outside seems to calm. LOGGINS sits down slowly, involuntarily, like a man being lowered into a warm bath.*

*HALL grips the back of a chair.*

*CROSS closes his eyes.*

*McDONALD's mouth opens slightly.*

---

**CROSBY:**
*(singing)*

Sailing a reach before a following sea...

---

*The salmon, cold now, forgotten, seems to glisten.*

*The shattered wine glass on the floor seems to reassemble itself. It doesn't actually reassemble itself. But it seems to.*

---

**CROSBY:**
*(singing)*

She was making for the trades on the outside...

---

*DUPREE is crying.*

*He doesn't know he's crying.*

*No one tells him.*

---

**CROSBY:**
*(singing)*

And the downhill run to Papeete...

---

*McDONALD feels something he hasn't felt in years.*

*It's not happiness.*

*It's not sadness.*

*It's the feeling of being on a boat.*

*He hasn't been on a boat in six years.*

*But he's on one now.*

*They all are.*

---

*CROSBY finishes.*

*The last chord hangs in the air.*

*It hangs for a very long time.*

*Longer than chords usually hang.*

*Then it fades.*

---

*Silence.*

---

**CROSBY:**

Well?

---

*No one speaks.*

---

**CROSBY:**

IS IT SMOOTH?

---

*McDONALD opens his mouth.*

*He closes it.*

*He opens it again.*

---

**McDONALD:**

It's...

**CROSBY:**

SAY IT.

**McDONALD:**

It's smooth, David.

**CROSBY:**

LOUDER.

**McDONALD:**

It's smooth.

**CROSBY:**

I CAN'T HEAR YOU, MICHAEL.

**McDONALD:**

IT'S SMOOTH, DAVID. IT'S INCREDIBLY SMOOTH. IT'S POSSIBLY THE SMOOTHEST SONG EVER WRITTEN. IT'S SO SMOOTH IT HURTS. IT'S SO SMOOTH I'VE SPENT FORTY YEARS PRETENDING IT DOESN'T EXIST BECAUSE IF I ACKNOWLEDGED IT I'D HAVE TO ACKNOWLEDGE THAT SOMEONE OUTSIDE OUR LITTLE CLUB ACHIEVED PERFECT SMOOTHNESS WITHOUT OUR PERMISSION AND THAT TERRIFIES ME.

---

*He's panting.*

*Everyone is staring.*

*CROSBY nods slowly.*

---

**CROSBY:**

That's all I wanted to hear.

---

*He puts the guitar back in the small case.*

*He puts the small case back in the big case.*

*He picks up the unlabeled bottle.*

*He takes a long drink.*

*He sets it down.*

---

**CROSBY:**

You can keep that.

**McDONALD:**

What is it?

**CROSBY:**

Better if you don't know.

---

*He walks to the door.*

*He stops.*

*He turns.*

---

**CROSBY:**

One more thing.

**McDONALD:**

Yes?

**CROSBY:**

I've slept with all of your wives.

---

*He leaves.*

*The door closes.*

---

*No one moves.*

---

**LOGGINS:**

...all of them?

**McDONALD:**

He's lying.

**HALL:**

Is he?

**McDONALD:**

He has to be.

**CROSS:**

*(looking at the closed door)*

Does he though?

---

*Silence.*

*A long, long silence.*

---

**DUPREE:**

So... can we add "Southern Cross" to the playlist now?

---

*McDONALD looks at the table.*

*The salmon.*

*The guitar case indentation in the salmon.*

*The wine.*

*The broken glass that did not reassemble itself.*

---

**McDONALD:**

...yes.

**DUPREE:**

Really?

**McDONALD:**

But we're never speaking of this dinner again.

**DUPREE:**

Agreed.

**McDONALD:**

And no one tells Steely Dan.

**EVERYONE:**

Agreed.

---

*FADE TO BLACK.*

---

*"His replacement liver outlived him by six hours."*

*"No one knows what happened to it."*

---

*One more line:*

*"Stephen Stills was not invited to the dinner."*

*"Neither was Graham Nash."*

*"They have not been told about any of this."*

*"They still don't know."*

**END OF EPISODE.**

---

*POST-CREDITS SCENE:*

*A different house. Laurel Canyon. Night.*

*GRAHAM NASH sits alone, reading a book about photography.*

*His phone buzzes.*

*He looks at it.*

*A text from an unknown number:*

**"They talked about the song."**

*Nash stares at the phone.*

*He sets down the book.*

*He picks up a landline telephone. Yes, he still has a landline. He's Graham Nash.*

*He dials.*

---

**NASH:**

Stephen. It's me. ... Yes, I know what time it is. ... Listen. Someone talked. ... Yes. THE song. ... No, I don't know who. ... We need to call a meeting. ... Not with them. With *us.* ... The old protocols. ... Yes, THOSE protocols.

*He hangs up.*

*He looks out the window.*

*The canyon is dark.*

*But somewhere out there, smooth or not, the music continues.*

---

**NASH:**

*(to himself)*

They thought we didn't know.

*(small smile)*

We always know.

---
