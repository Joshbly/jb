**YACHT ROCK**

**Episode II: "Porcaro"**

---

*FADE IN:*

*A hallway. Long. Too long. The kind of long that only exists in dreams and Los Angeles recording studios in 1982.*

*Fluorescent lights hum.*

*One of them flickers.*

*It has always flickered.*

*It will never be fixed.*

---

*DAVID PAICH walks down the hallway. He is the keyboardist for Toto. He is wearing a shirt that costs more than your car. He does not know this. He has never known the price of anything. Things simply appear in his life, smooth and paid for.*

*He is carrying sheet music.*

*The sheet music is for a song called "Rosanna."*

*He does not know yet what this song will become.*

*None of them know.*

*Except one.*

---

*Paich reaches a door. On the door is a piece of tape. On the tape, written in Sharpie, is one word:*

**PORCARO**

*Paich hesitates.*

*He knocks.*

*Nothing.*

*He knocks again.*

---

**VOICE FROM INSIDE:**

It's open.

---

*Paich enters.*

*The room is dark except for a single lamp illuminating a drum kit. The drum kit gleams. It is perfectly tuned. Each head is tensioned to within .003 millimeters of absolute smoothness. This is not possible. And yet.*

*Seated at the kit is JEFF PORCARO.*

*He is not moving.*

*He is just sitting.*

*His sticks rest across his lap like a saint holding a sword in a painting from the Renaissance, the ones where everyone looks calm but you know violence is coming.*

---

**PAICH:**

Jeff.

**PORCARO:**

David.

**PAICH:**

I brought the charts for "Rosanna."

**PORCARO:**

I know.

**PAICH:**

How do you—

**PORCARO:**

I know everything that enters this building, David.

*He taps a cymbal. Once. The sound lasts for eleven seconds. This is too long. Cymbals don't do this. This one does.*

**PORCARO:**

I felt the song when you wrote it. Thursday. 3:47 AM. You were eating cold pasta.

**PAICH:**
*(pale now)*

That's... that's correct.

**PORCARO:**

The pasta was orecchiette.

**PAICH:**

Jeff, how—

**PORCARO:**

*The song told me, David.*

---

*Paich sets the sheet music on a stand. His hands are trembling. Porcaro does not look at the music. He doesn't need to. The music is already inside him. It has always been inside him. He was born with "Rosanna" encoded in his DNA, waiting.*

---

**PAICH:**

The label wants something upbeat. Something with groove. Something—

**PORCARO:**

They want smoothness.

**PAICH:**

Well, yes, but also—

**PORCARO:**

There is no "also." There is only smoothness. Smoothness is not a quality, David. It is not an adjective. It is a *state of being.* You either exist in smoothness or you exist outside of it, cold and alone, making... 

*He pauses. His face twists with something like nausea.*

**PORCARO:**

...making *prog rock.*

**PAICH:**
*(crossing himself)*

God forbid.

**PORCARO:**

God has nothing to do with this.

---

*Porcaro stands. He is shorter than you'd expect. All the great ones are. Napoleon. Prince. This man who is about to invent the most iconic drum groove of the 1980s.*

*He approaches Paich.*

*He takes the sheet music.*

*He does not read it.*

*He smells it.*

---

**PORCARO:**

Who is Rosanna?

**PAICH:**

She's... she's a woman. A woman I loved.

**PORCARO:**

Wrong.

**PAICH:**

I'm... what?

**PORCARO:**

Rosanna is not a woman. Rosanna is an *idea.* Rosanna is the concept of longing made flesh. Rosanna is what happens when a man reaches for something smooth and it slips away, like trying to hold water, like trying to keep a perfect moment from ending.

*He hands the sheet music back.*

**PORCARO:**

Rosanna is all of us, David.

*Paich is crying now. He doesn't know why. Neither do we.*

**PAICH:**

Can you... can you play it?

---

*Porcaro returns to his kit.*

*He sits.*

*He closes his eyes.*

*Thirty seconds pass.*

*Forty-five.*

*A minute.*

*Paich does not move. He cannot move. He is witnessing something. He does not know what yet.*

---

*And then.*

*Porcaro plays.*

---

*The groove is—*

*How do you describe it?*

*It is a half-time shuffle.*

*But it's more than that.*

*It's a conversation between the hi-hat and the snare. The hi-hat says "I love you." The snare says "I know, but we can never be together." The kick drum says "Time is passing, and we are all dying." The ghost notes—oh, the ghost notes—the ghost notes say nothing. They are ghosts. They haunt the spaces between the beats, present and absent simultaneously, like memories of someone you loved who has moved on.*

*Paich drops the sheet music.*

*Somewhere in the building, a secretary starts weeping at her desk. She doesn't know why. She will never know why.*

*In a bathroom three floors up, a session guitarist looks in the mirror and whispers: "I need to call my father."*

*Outside, a bird lands on the roof, listens for a moment, and flies away changed.*

---

*Porcaro stops playing.*

*Silence.*

*The silence after smoothness is different from regular silence. It has weight. It has texture. It remembers what came before.*

---

**PAICH:**
*(barely audible)*

Jeff.

**PORCARO:**

Yes.

**PAICH:**

What... what was that?

**PORCARO:**

That was the Rosanna shuffle.

**PAICH:**

I've never heard anything like—

**PORCARO:**

No one has. No one will again. Other drummers will try to play it. They will practice for years. Decades. They will watch videos. They will slow it down frame by frame. They will *think* they understand.

*He picks up his sticks. He examines them like a surgeon examining a scalpel, or a priest examining a communion wafer.*

**PORCARO:**

They won't understand.

**PAICH:**

Why not?

**PORCARO:**

Because they're playing the *notes,* David.

*He looks up. For the first time, he makes eye contact. His eyes are the color of aged bourbon. No. Darker. His eyes are the color of the space between stars where light has given up.*

**PORCARO:**

I'm playing the *spaces between the notes.*

---

*SMASH CUT TO:*

*A DIFFERENT STUDIO. Later. The full band is assembled. STEVE LUKATHER is holding a guitar. He looks nervous. Everyone looks nervous. They are about to record "Rosanna." They know something is happening. They don't know what.*

*Porcaro sits at his kit.*

*He is perfectly still.*

*A fly lands on his arm.*

*He does not move.*

*The fly leaves, unnerved.*

---

**PRODUCER (V.O.):**

Okay, "Rosanna," take one.

*The tape rolls.*

*Porcaro counts in.*

*He does not count out loud.*

*He counts with his breath.*

*Everyone feels it.*

*They begin.*

---

*The groove hits and—*

*Look.*

*I need you to understand something.*

*The Rosanna shuffle is not a drum beat. The Rosanna shuffle is a proof of God's existence submitted directly to the ears, bypassing all rational thought. Philosophers have spent centuries constructing elaborate arguments for the divine. Porcaro did it in four bars.*

*The hi-hat is doing something impossible. It is playing in between the spaces that shouldn't exist. It is dividing time into fractions that mathematics has not yet named. Somewhere, a calculus professor feels a chill and doesn't know why.*

---

*The song ends.*

*Silence.*

*Then:*

---

**LUKATHER:**

Jeff.

**PORCARO:**

Yes.

**LUKATHER:**

I think I need to sit down.

**PORCARO:**

You are sitting down.

**LUKATHER:**

I think I need to sit down *more.*

---

*He slides off his chair onto the floor.*

*No one helps him.*

*They understand.*

---

*CUT TO:*

*Later. Much later. The studio is empty. Everyone has gone home. The lights are off except for the exit sign, glowing red like the eye of something that watches but does not judge.*

*Porcaro is still at his kit.*

*He has not moved.*

*He will not move until the next session.*

*This is his home now.*

*This has always been his home.*

---

*He plays the groove again. Alone. For no one. For everyone.*

---

*FADE TO BLACK.*

*TEXT ON SCREEN:*

*"'Rosanna' won the Grammy for Record of the Year in 1983."*

*"Jeff Porcaro died in 1992 at the age of 38."*

*"The groove remains."*

*"The groove will always remain."*

---

*One more line of text:*

*"No one has ever truly replicated the Rosanna shuffle."*

*"Many have tried."*

*"All have played the notes."*

*"None have played the spaces."*

---

*CREDITS.*

*No music plays.*

*Music would be disrespectful.*

*The silence is the tribute.*

---

**END OF EPISODE.**

---

*POST-CREDITS SCENE:*

*A drum shop. Present day. A TEENAGER is practicing. He is trying to play the Rosanna shuffle. He's watched seventeen YouTube tutorials. He's got the sticking down. He's *close.* So close.*

*An OLD MAN enters the shop. He walks slowly. He wears a faded Toto t-shirt. He watches the teenager play.*

*The teenager finishes. Looks up. Hopeful.*

---

**TEENAGER:**

How was that?

*The old man is silent for a long moment.*

**OLD MAN:**

You played all the notes, kid.

**TEENAGER:**

...thanks?

**OLD MAN:**

That wasn't a compliment.

*He turns and walks out.*

*The teenager looks at his hands.*

*He will practice for another ten thousand hours.*

*He will never get there.*

*None of them ever get there.*
