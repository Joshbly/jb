**YACHT ROCK**

**Episode IV: "The Certification"**

---

*FADE IN:*

*A warehouse. Somewhere in Los Angeles. The address is unlisted. The building has no windows. The parking lot has one car in it: a 1977 Chrysler Cordoba, burgundy, immaculate.*

*Inside the warehouse: a single folding chair.*

*In the chair sits STEVE PERRY.*

*Yes. That Steve Perry.*

*He is blindfolded.*

*He does not know why he is blindfolded. He asked forty-five minutes ago. No one answered. He has not asked again.*

---

*A door opens.*

*Footsteps.*

*Two sets.*

*In perfect synchronization.*

---

*DONALD FAGEN and WALTER BECKER enter. They are wearing lab coats. The lab coats are black. You didn't know lab coats came in black. Neither did anyone else. Steely Dan had them made.*

---

**FAGEN:**

Remove his blindfold.

*BECKER removes the blindfold.*

*PERRY blinks.*

**PERRY:**

Where am I?

**FAGEN:**

You're in the Evaluation Center.

**PERRY:**

The what?

**BECKER:**

The Evaluation Center.

**PERRY:**

I heard him. I'm asking what that *means.*

**FAGEN:**

It means you're being evaluated.

**PERRY:**

For what?

**BECKER:**

Smoothness.

---

*PERRY looks around the warehouse. There is nothing in it except the chair, a card table with a pitcher of water, and a massive poster of a boat. The boat is not labeled. It is just a boat.*

---

**PERRY:**

I'm in Journey.

**FAGEN:**

We know.

**PERRY:**

Journey sells out stadiums.

**BECKER:**

We know.

**PERRY:**

I sang "Don't Stop Believin'."

**FAGEN:**

We're aware.

**PERRY:**

That song has been played at every wedding, bar mitzvah, and divorce proceeding in America since 1981.

**BECKER:**

And yet.

**PERRY:**

And yet *what?*

---

*Fagen walks to the card table. He pours a glass of water. He does not drink it. He just holds it.*

---

**FAGEN:**

Steve, do you know why you're here?

**PERRY:**

You *kidnapped* me.

**FAGEN:**

We *invited* you.

**PERRY:**

From a grocery store parking lot. With a bag over my head.

**BECKER:**

The bag was Egyptian cotton, Steve. Four-hundred thread count. We're not *animals.*

---

**PERRY:**

I want to leave.

**FAGEN:**

You can leave whenever you want.

**PERRY:**

Good.

*He stands.*

**FAGEN:**

But if you leave, you'll never be smooth.

*PERRY stops.*

**FAGEN:**

You'll spend the rest of your career in arenas, Steve. Arenas full of people holding lighters and swaying. Do you know what swaying is, Steve?

**PERRY:**

It's—

**FAGEN:**

Swaying is almost smooth. Swaying is smooth's cousin who didn't get into the good college. Swaying is what happens when people *want* to be moved but don't know *how.* You've given them swaying for twenty years. Don't you want to give them *stillness?*

---

*PERRY sits back down.*

---

**PERRY:**

What do I have to do?

---

*BECKER smiles. The smile does not reach his eyes. His smiles never reach his eyes. His eyes are for other purposes.*

---

**BECKER:**

There are seven trials.

**PERRY:**

Seven?

**FAGEN:**

Seven is the smoothest number.

**PERRY:**

I thought that was—

**BECKER:**

You thought wrong.

---

*Becker pulls a small notebook from his lab coat. The notebook is leather. The leather is smooth. Of course it is.*

---

**BECKER:**

Trial One: The Tempo Test.

*He claps his hands.*

*A METRONOME descends from the ceiling on a wire. It is already ticking.*

**BECKER:**

What tempo is this?

*PERRY listens.*

**PERRY:**

Uh... 120?

*Silence.*

**FAGEN:**

He said 120.

**BECKER:**

I heard.

**FAGEN:**

120 is a *running* tempo, Steve.

**BECKER:**

120 is what they play at gyms.

**FAGEN:**

120 is the tempo of a man who has somewhere to be.

**BECKER:**

Yacht rock has nowhere to be, Steve.

**FAGEN:**

Yacht rock is already there.

**PERRY:**

It sounded like 120.

**BECKER:**

It's 94.

**PERRY:**

That's... that's basically the same—

**FAGEN:**

BASICALLY?

*The word echoes through the warehouse.*

**FAGEN:**

There is no "basically" in smoothness, Steve. There is only *exactly.* Basically is for people who eat at chain restaurants. Basically is for people who say "close enough." Do you know what happens when a yacht hits a dock at "basically" the right speed?

**PERRY:**

It—

**FAGEN:**

FIBERGLASS EVERYWHERE, STEVE.

---

*PERRY is sweating now.*

---

**BECKER:**

Trial Two.

*He claps again.*

*A SECOND CHAIR descends from the ceiling. In the chair is MICHAEL McDONALD. He is also blindfolded.*

**McDONALD:**

Hello?

**FAGEN:**

Michael.

**McDONALD:**

Donald. Walter.

*He removes his own blindfold. He has clearly been here before.*

**McDONALD:**

Who's the candidate?

**BECKER:**

Steve Perry.

*McDONALD looks at Perry.*

*Perry looks at McDonald.*

**McDONALD:**

Journey?

**PERRY:**

Journey.

**McDONALD:**

Hm.

**PERRY:**

What does "hm" mean?

**McDONALD:**

It means "hm," Steve.

---

**FAGEN:**

Michael, please administer the Harmony Evaluation.

*McDonald nods. He clears his throat. He begins to sing.*

**McDONALD:**
*(singing)*

What a fool believes...

*He gestures to Perry.*

*Perry joins in.*

**PERRY:**
*(singing)*

He sees...

*McDonald stops.*

*Everyone stops.*

**McDONALD:**

No.

**PERRY:**

What?

**McDONALD:**

You sang it like you were in a *stadium.*

**PERRY:**

I *am* usually in a stadium.

**McDONALD:**

Yacht rock isn't *in* anything, Steve. Yacht rock *is* the thing. You don't sing *to* people. You sing *near* people. You let the sound happen *around* them like weather. Like fog. Have you ever been in fog, Steve?

**PERRY:**

I'm from San Francisco.

**McDONALD:**

Then you should *know.*

---

**BECKER:**

He failed the harmony test.

**FAGEN:**

Note it.

*Becker writes in his notebook.*

**PERRY:**

Can I try again?

**FAGEN:**

There are no second chances in smoothness, Steve.

**PERRY:**

That seems harsh.

**FAGEN:**

Smoothness *is* harsh. Smoothness is the harshest thing there is. You know why? Because it looks easy. It looks like nothing. And that nothing took *years* to achieve. That nothing cost us our twenties, Steve. Our thirties. Most of Walter's first marriage.

**BECKER:**

Both marriages.

**FAGEN:**

Both marriages?

**BECKER:**

I don't want to talk about it.

**FAGEN:**

We're talking about it later.

**BECKER:**

Fine.

---

**FAGEN:**

Trial Three.

*He claps.*

*A SCREEN descends. On the screen: a photograph of a boat.*

**FAGEN:**

What kind of boat is this?

*PERRY squints.*

**PERRY:**

A... sailboat?

**FAGEN:**

What *kind* of sailboat?

**PERRY:**

A... white one?

---

*McDONALD puts his head in his hands.*

---

**BECKER:**

It's a Catalina 30 MkII, Steve.

**PERRY:**

How was I supposed to—

**BECKER:**

You were supposed to *know.* A yacht rocker knows boats, Steve. A yacht rocker can identify a vessel by the sound of its hull against the water. A yacht rocker can smell the difference between teak and mahogany from forty yards.

**PERRY:**

That's insane.

**FAGEN:**

That's *smooth.*

---

*The screen changes. A new photograph. A different boat.*

---

**FAGEN:**

What about this one?

**PERRY:**

I don't—

**FAGEN:**

It's a TRICK, Steve. That's not a boat. That's a large duck. In the fog. You would've sailed toward it thinking it was a vessel and RAMMED A DUCK, STEVE.

**PERRY:**

Why would I be sailing toward anything? I'm a singer!

**FAGEN:**

A singer who wants to be SMOOTH.

**BECKER:**

And smooth means BOATS.

**FAGEN:**

SMOOTH MEANS BOATS, STEVE.

---

*Perry is shaking.*

---

**McDONALD:**

Should we break?

**FAGEN:**

There are no breaks in the Evaluation.

**McDONALD:**

He looks like he might cry.

**BECKER:**

Crying is not smooth.

**McDONALD:**

I cried during the recording of "I Keep Forgettin'."

**FAGEN:**

That was different.

**McDONALD:**

How?

**FAGEN:**

You cried *in key.*

---

*A door at the far end of the warehouse opens.*

*In walks KENNY LOGGINS.*

*He is carrying a clipboard.*

*The clipboard has papers on it.*

---

**LOGGINS:**

Sorry I'm late.

**FAGEN:**

You're not part of this, Kenny.

**LOGGINS:**

I'm the Smoothness Ombudsman now.

**BECKER:**

Since when?

**LOGGINS:**

Since the Marina Del Rey incident. It's part of my probation.

**FAGEN:**

Who approved this?

**LOGGINS:**

The Council.

**FAGEN:**

There's no Council.

**LOGGINS:**

There's been a Council since March, Donald. You'd know that if you attended the meetings.

**FAGEN:**

We don't attend meetings.

**BECKER:**

Meetings are not smooth.

**LOGGINS:**

The meetings are smooth. I've made them smooth.

**FAGEN:**

You can't *make* something smooth, Kenny. You can only *remove what is unsmooth.*

**LOGGINS:**

That's the same thing.

**FAGEN:**

IT IS PROFOUNDLY NOT THE SAME THING.

---

*Perry raises his hand.*

---

**PERRY:**

Can I... can I say something?

*Everyone turns.*

**PERRY:**

I wrote "Open Arms."

*Silence.*

**PERRY:**

I wrote "Faithfully."

*More silence.*

**PERRY:**

I wrote "Who's Crying Now."

**BECKER:**

And?

**PERRY:**

Those are love songs.

**FAGEN:**

Those are *power ballads,* Steve.

**PERRY:**

What's the difference?

---

*Fagen walks to Perry. He kneels. He takes Perry's hands. Perry's hands are warm. Fagen's hands are room temperature. Fagen's hands are always room temperature. No one knows why.*

---

**FAGEN:**

Steve.

A love song is a question.

A power ballad is an *answer.*

Yacht rock is neither. Yacht rock is the *pause between the question and the answer.* Yacht rock is the moment before you say something you can't take back. Yacht rock is the breath.

Do you understand?

**PERRY:**

I... think so?

**FAGEN:**

You don't.

*He stands.*

**FAGEN:**

Trial Four.

*He claps.*

---

*Nothing descends from the ceiling.*

*Instead, a portion of the floor opens.*

*Rising from below: TOTO.*

*ALL OF TOTO.*

*On a hydraulic platform.*

*They are playing "Africa."*

*They do not stop playing when they reach floor level.*

*They just keep playing.*

---

**FAGEN:**

*(shouting over the music)*

STOP THEM.

**PERRY:**

WHAT?

**FAGEN:**

MAKE THEM STOP. THEY'LL ONLY STOP IF SOMEONE SMOOTH TELLS THEM TO.

**PERRY:**

HOW DO I—

**FAGEN:**

YOU HAVE TO FEEL IT, STEVE.

**BECKER:**

*(also shouting)*

FEEL THE SMOOTH, STEVE.

**LOGGINS:**

*(checking his clipboard)*

THIS ISN'T IN THE PROTOCOL.

**FAGEN:**

THE PROTOCOL IS WHATEVER WE SAY IT IS.

---

*PERRY stands.*

*He walks toward Toto.*

*They are still playing. The drums are thunderous. The keyboards are cascading. STEVE LUKATHER is doing a guitar solo that has been going for ninety seconds and shows no signs of stopping.*

*Perry raises his hand.*

---

**PERRY:**

STOP!

*They do not stop.*

**PERRY:**

PLEASE STOP!

*They play louder.*

*LUKATHER adds a second guitar.*

*No one knows where the second guitar came from.*

---

*Perry turns to Fagen.*

---

**PERRY:**

THEY'RE NOT STOPPING.

**FAGEN:**

YOU'RE NOT BEING SMOOTH, STEVE.

**PERRY:**

I'M YELLING. YELLING IS NOT SMOOTH.

**FAGEN:**

THEN DON'T YELL.

**PERRY:**

I CAN'T HEAR MYSELF IF I DON'T YELL.

**BECKER:**

SMOOTH DOESN'T NEED TO HEAR ITSELF, STEVE. SMOOTH JUST *IS.*

---

*Perry closes his eyes.*

*He breathes.*

*He doesn't yell.*

*He simply... speaks.*

---

**PERRY:**
*(almost a whisper)*

Stop.

---

*TOTO STOPS.*

*Instantly.*

*LUKATHER's hand freezes mid-solo. PAICH's fingers hover over the keys. The drummer—a new guy, not Porcaro, never Porcaro, no one is Porcaro—stops with his sticks in the air.*

---

*Silence.*

---

**FAGEN:**

Hm.

**BECKER:**

Hm.

**McDONALD:**

That was almost smooth.

**LOGGINS:**

*(writing on his clipboard)*

I'm noting this as a partial success.

**FAGEN:**

You don't note things, Kenny.

**LOGGINS:**

I note things now. I'm the Ombudsman.

**FAGEN:**

You're a man with a clipboard who used to be smooth and is now only *smooth-adjacent.*

**LOGGINS:**

That's hurtful.

**FAGEN:**

Smooth is often hurtful, Kenny. Smooth doesn't care about your feelings. Smooth only cares about *itself.*

---

*The Toto platform begins to descend.*

*LUKATHER waves as they go down.*

*No one waves back.*

*The floor closes.*

---

**FAGEN:**

Final trial.

**PERRY:**

I thought there were seven.

**FAGEN:**

I lied. There are five. Seven isn't the smoothest number. Five is.

**PERRY:**

You said—

**FAGEN:**

I say a lot of things, Steve. Part of being smooth is knowing when I'm lying.

**PERRY:**

How would I know that?

**FAGEN:**

You wouldn't. That's the point.

---

*He claps.*

*From the shadows emerges CHRISTOPHER CROSS.*

*He is carrying a single sheet of paper.*

*The paper is blank.*

---

**CROSS:**

Steve.

**PERRY:**

Christopher.

**CROSS:**

I need you to sign this.

**PERRY:**

What is it?

**CROSS:**

It's your commitment.

**PERRY:**

To what?

**CROSS:**

To smoothness. To the marina. To the eternal brotherhood of men who have chosen tempo over urgency, keys over distortion, the soft over the loud.

**PERRY:**

It's blank.

**CROSS:**

The commitment is blank because you fill it in, Steve. Every day. With your choices.

**PERRY:**

That's very abstract.

**CROSS:**

Smooth is abstract. Smooth is the most abstract thing there is. You can't touch smooth. You can't see smooth. You can only *be* smooth. And being smooth means signing a blank piece of paper and trusting that the paper knows what it needs from you.

---

*Perry looks at the paper.*

*He looks at Fagen.*

*He looks at Becker.*

*He looks at McDonald, who nods slightly.*

*He looks at Loggins, who is still writing on his clipboard.*

*He looks at Cross, who is waiting.*

---

**PERRY:**

If I sign this... I'm yacht rock?

**FAGEN:**

If you sign this, you're *eligible* to be yacht rock.

**PERRY:**

What's the difference?

**BECKER:**

About seven years of additional evaluation.

**PERRY:**

SEVEN YEARS?

**FAGEN:**

You can leave anytime, Steve. Go back to Journey. Go back to the stadiums. Go back to the lighters and the swaying and the "Don't Stop Believin'" at wedding receptions while drunk uncles cry.

**PERRY:**

What's wrong with drunk uncles crying?

**FAGEN:**

Nothing, Steve. Absolutely nothing. But drunk uncles don't buy yachts. Drunk uncles *rent* yachts. Drunk uncles take booze cruises in Cabo and call it "sailing." Is that what you want? To be the soundtrack of a booze cruise?

---

*Perry's hand trembles.*

*He reaches for a pen.*

*There is no pen.*

---

**PERRY:**

Where's the pen?

**CROSS:**

There is no pen.

**PERRY:**

How do I sign?

**CROSS:**

You don't sign with a pen, Steve.

**PERRY:**

Then how—

**CROSS:**

You sign with your *presence.*

---

*Perry stares at the blank paper.*

*The paper stares back.*

*He places his hand on it.*

*Nothing happens.*

*Five seconds.*

*Ten.*

*Then:*

*The paper begins to glow.*

*Softly.*

*Warmly.*

*Smoothly.*

---

**FAGEN:**

It's accepting him.

**BECKER:**

Hm.

**McDONALD:**

Welcome, Steve.

**LOGGINS:**

I'm noting this as a full success.

**FAGEN:**

Shut up, Kenny.

---

*The glow fades.*

*Perry lifts his hand.*

*On the paper, where there was nothing, there is now a single word:*

**PROVISIONAL**

---

**PERRY:**

Provisional?

**FAGEN:**

You didn't think it would say "yes," did you?

**PERRY:**

I hoped—

**BECKER:**

Hope is not smooth, Steve.

**FAGEN:**

Smooth is certainty. And you are not certain yet. You are provisional. You will be provisional for a long time. Maybe forever.

**PERRY:**

How do I become... not provisional?

**FAGEN:**

You stop asking.

---

*Fagen and Becker turn.*

*They walk toward the door.*

*McDonald follows.*

*Loggins follows McDonald, still writing.*

*Cross remains.*

---

**CROSS:**

Steve.

**PERRY:**

Yeah?

**CROSS:**

I was provisional for nine years.

**PERRY:**

But you're... you're *Christopher Cross.*

**CROSS:**

I know. And it still took nine years.

**PERRY:**

What changed?

**CROSS:**

Nothing. That's the secret. Nothing changes. You just wake up one day and you're smooth. Or you don't. And you never know which day it'll be. So you just keep waking up.

---

*He turns.*

*He walks away.*

*Perry sits alone in the warehouse.*

*The poster of the boat watches him.*

*It is still just a boat.*

*But somehow, now, it feels like more.*

---

*FADE TO BLACK.*

*TEXT ON SCREEN:*

*"Steve Perry remained provisional for eleven years."*

*"He never officially became yacht rock."*

*"Neither did Journey."*

*"They continue to sell out arenas."*

*"The arenas remain unsmooth."*

---

*One more line:*

*"The paper is still in the warehouse."*

*"It still says 'PROVISIONAL.'"*

*"No one has moved it."*

*"No one knows if they're allowed."*

---

**END OF EPISODE.**

*The Evaluation Center. Empty. Night.*

*The door opens.*

*A figure enters.*

*It is EDDIE MONEY.*

*He looks around.*

*He sees the chair.*

*He sits.*

*He waits.*

---

*No one comes.*

*No one ever comes for Eddie Money.*

---

*He sits there for six hours.*

*Then he leaves.*

*He does not come back.*
