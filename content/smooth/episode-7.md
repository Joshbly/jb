number: 7
title: The Origin
preview: A therapist's office in Santa Monica. Christopher Cross on the leather couch. His eyes are closed. He is about to remember everything.
---

*FADE IN:*

*A therapist's office. Santa Monica. The walls are beige. The couch is leather. The leather is smooth. Of course it is.*

*On the couch: CHRISTOPHER CROSS.*

*He is lying down. His eyes are closed. His hands are folded on his chest like a man in a coffin, but alive, technically.*

*In a chair across from him: DR. ELLEN WEISS. She is sixty-three. She has been a therapist for thirty-one years. She has treated actors, directors, one astronaut, and now this.*

*She does not know what "this" is.*

*She is beginning to suspect no one does.*

---

**DR. WEISS:**

Christopher, you've been coming here for six months.

**CROSS:**

Yes.

**DR. WEISS:**

And in six months, we've discussed your childhood, your career, your Grammy wins, your subsequent obscurity, your relationship with anonymity, and your feelings about the word "smooth."

**CROSS:**

Yes.

**DR. WEISS:**

But there's something we haven't discussed.

**CROSS:**

Is there?

**DR. WEISS:**

"Sailing."

---

*CROSS's eyes open.*

*He sits up.*

*He has never sat up before.*

*In six months, he has never once sat up.*

---

**CROSS:**

I don't talk about "Sailing."

**DR. WEISS:**

Why not?

**CROSS:**

Because "Sailing" talks about itself.

**DR. WEISS:**

What does that mean?

**CROSS:**

It means the song doesn't need me. It never needed me. I was just the vessel. The... the *delivery system.*

**DR. WEISS:**

You wrote it.

**CROSS:**

Did I?

**DR. WEISS:**

Christopher, you won a Grammy for it. Song of the Year. 1981.

**CROSS:**

I know what year it was, Dr. Weiss. I know what I won. But winning something doesn't mean you understand it. I've spent forty-four years with that song, and I still don't know where it came from.

---

*He stands.*

*He walks to the window.*

*Outside: palm trees. A parking lot. A man walking a small dog. The man is wearing sandals. The dog is not. Dogs don't wear sandals. This is normal.*

---

**CROSS:**

Do you know what people say about "Sailing"?

**DR. WEISS:**

What do they say?

**CROSS:**

They say it's the smoothest song ever written. They say it invented yacht rock. They say it's the *template.* The *ur-text.* Every soft rock song with a boat metaphor, every gentle production with synthesized warmth, every lyric about escape and water and being somewhere other than where you are... they all trace back to "Sailing."

**DR. WEISS:**

That sounds like a significant achievement.

**CROSS:**

It sounds like a CURSE, Dr. Weiss.

---

*He turns.*

---

**CROSS:**

Do you know what it's like to peak at twenty-nine? To write something so perfect, so *complete,* that everything after it is just... aftermath? I've released eleven albums. ELEVEN. And no one cares. No one will ever care. Because I already wrote the song. THE song. The one that matters. And now I'm just... living in its shadow.

**DR. WEISS:**

Christopher, many artists struggle with—

**CROSS:**

I'm not struggling with success, Dr. Weiss. I'm struggling with *origin.* I'm struggling with the fact that I don't know if I wrote "Sailing" or if "Sailing" wrote *me.*

---

*DR. WEISS sets down her pen.*

*This is unusual.*

*She always holds the pen.*

*The pen is her anchor.*

*Without the pen, she is adrift.*

---

**DR. WEISS:**

Explain that.

**CROSS:**

I was in my apartment. 1979. San Antonio. I had a keyboard. A four-track. A window that looked out at nothing—just a parking lot, like that one, except worse. I was nobody. I had written songs before, but they were just... songs. Words and notes. The usual arrangement.

And then one night, I sat down, and I played a chord. And the chord was... different. It was like it had been waiting. Like it existed before I played it and I just happened to be the one who found it.

**DR. WEISS:**

What chord was it?

**CROSS:**

I don't know how to describe it. It was like... have you ever walked into a room you've never been in before, but it felt like home? Like you belonged there, even though you'd never seen it?

**DR. WEISS:**

Yes.

**CROSS:**

It was that. But in sound form.

---

*He sits back down on the couch.*

*He does not lie down.*

*Progress.*

---

**CROSS:**

I played the chord, and then I played another one. And another. And they just... fit. Like they'd always been together. Like I was reassembling something that had been broken, putting the pieces back where they belonged.

And then I started singing.

But it wasn't like normal singing. It wasn't like choosing words. It was like... *remembering* words. Words I'd never known but somehow already knew.

**DR. WEISS:**

What were the words?

**CROSS:**

You know what they were.

**DR. WEISS:**

Tell me anyway.

**CROSS:**

They were about sailing. About being free. About escaping into the water and the wind and becoming something other than yourself.

But here's the thing, Dr. Weiss:

I had never been sailing.

**DR. WEISS:**

Never?

**CROSS:**

Not once. Not even on a lake. I grew up in San Antonio. San Antonio is landlocked. The closest body of water was a municipal pool that closed in 1974 because of a bacterial issue.

**DR. WEISS:**

So how did you write a song about sailing?

**CROSS:**

That's what I'm trying to tell you. I didn't write it. It wrote itself. THROUGH me. I was just the... the *antenna.* The *receiver.* Something was broadcasting, and I happened to be tuned to the right frequency.

---

*DR. WEISS picks up her pen again.*

*The pen is back.*

*Order is restored.*

---

**DR. WEISS:**

Christopher, have you considered that you might simply be a talented songwriter who tapped into a universal feeling?

**CROSS:**

I've considered it.

**DR. WEISS:**

And?

**CROSS:**

And it doesn't explain the boat.

**DR. WEISS:**

What boat?

---

*CROSS is quiet for a long moment.*

---

**CROSS:**

Three days after I recorded "Sailing"—the demo, just me and the four-track—I got a call. A man. He didn't give his name. He just said: "I heard the song."

I said, "What song? I haven't played it for anyone."

He said, "You played it for everyone. You just don't know it yet."

And then he said, "There's a boat waiting for you. Slip 22-B. Marina del Rey. It's yours now."

And he hung up.

**DR. WEISS:**

Did you go?

**CROSS:**

Of course I went.

**DR. WEISS:**

And?

**CROSS:**

There was a boat. A sailboat. A Catalina 30 MkII, pristine, like it had never touched water. The registration was in my name. The keys were in the ignition. There was a note on the steering wheel.

**DR. WEISS:**

What did the note say?

**CROSS:**

It said: "You didn't write the song. The song wrote the boat. The boat was always waiting. Now it's yours. Don't ask questions. Just sail."

**DR. WEISS:**

And did you?

**CROSS:**

I've never stopped.

---

*Silence.*

*DR. WEISS writes something on her pad.*

*She writes for a long time.*

*Cross watches her.*

---

**CROSS:**

You think I'm crazy.

**DR. WEISS:**

I think you're processing a very unusual experience.

**CROSS:**

There's more.

**DR. WEISS:**

Tell me.

---

**CROSS:**

After "Sailing" came out—after it went to number one, after the Grammys, after all of it—I started meeting other people. Musicians. Soft rock guys. The ones who'd been doing it for years before me.

And they all said the same thing.

They said "Sailing" changed everything.

They said before "Sailing," they were just making music. Pop. Rock. Whatever. But after "Sailing," there was a *template.* A *blueprint.* Suddenly there was a word for what they were doing. Suddenly there was a *genre.*

**DR. WEISS:**

Yacht rock.

**CROSS:**

Exactly. But here's what no one understands, Dr. Weiss. Here's what keeps me up at night:

I didn't invent yacht rock.

Yacht rock invented ME.

**DR. WEISS:**

What do you mean?

**CROSS:**

I mean the genre existed before the song. The feeling existed before the genre. The boat existed before I knew it was there. Everything was already in place, waiting, like a room that had been prepared for a guest who hadn't arrived yet.

And then I arrived.

And I played the chord.

And everything... *crystallized.*

**DR. WEISS:**

So you're saying "Sailing" didn't create yacht rock. You're saying it... *revealed* it?

**CROSS:**

I'm saying "Sailing" is the moment the universe became smooth. And I don't know if that makes me a genius or a puppet.

---

*The clock on the wall ticks.*

*It is the only sound.*

---

**DR. WEISS:**

Christopher, I want to try something.

**CROSS:**

What?

**DR. WEISS:**

I want you to close your eyes.

**CROSS:**

Okay.

*He closes his eyes.*

**DR. WEISS:**

I want you to imagine you're on the boat. The boat from Slip 22-B. You're sailing. It's sunset. The water is calm.

**CROSS:**

I'm there.

**DR. WEISS:**

What do you hear?

**CROSS:**

Nothing. Just the water. The wind.

**DR. WEISS:**

No music?

**CROSS:**

...

**DR. WEISS:**

Christopher?

**CROSS:**

There's no music.

**DR. WEISS:**

Why not?

---

*Cross opens his eyes.*

*They are wet.*

---

**CROSS:**

Because I AM the music.

That's the secret. That's what I've never told anyone. When I'm on the boat—the real boat, MY boat—"Sailing" doesn't play. It doesn't need to. Because I'm inside it. I'm living it. The song isn't a recording. It's a *state of being.* And when I'm in that state, there's no difference between me and the song, between the boat and the water, between the wind and the melody.

I don't HEAR "Sailing."

I BECOME it.

**DR. WEISS:**

And when you're not on the boat?

**CROSS:**

Then I'm just Christopher Cross. A man who won some Grammys in 1981 and has spent the rest of his life trying to remember what it felt like to be something more.

---

*Silence.*

*DR. WEISS sets down her pen.*

*She leans forward.*

---

**DR. WEISS:**

Christopher, I'm going to tell you something that I probably shouldn't tell you.

**CROSS:**

What?

**DR. WEISS:**

My husband and I got married in 1983. Our first dance was to "Sailing."

**CROSS:**

A lot of people danced to "Sailing."

**DR. WEISS:**

I know. But when we danced to it—when I closed my eyes and listened—I wasn't in the reception hall anymore. I was on a boat. I was on the water. I was somewhere else entirely.

That's not a delusion, Christopher. That's not you being "just a vessel." That's you giving people something real. Something that TRANSPORTS them.

You didn't just write a song. You created a *place.* A place people can go when they need to be somewhere other than where they are.

And that place will exist forever.

Long after you're gone.

Long after all of us are gone.

The boat will still be there.

And someone will always be sailing.

---

*CROSS stares at her.*

*For a long moment, he doesn't speak.*

*Then:*

---

**CROSS:**

That's the nicest thing anyone has ever said to me.

**DR. WEISS:**

It's also true.

**CROSS:**

How do you know?

**DR. WEISS:**

Because I'm there right now.

*She closes her eyes.*

On the boat.

---

*The clock stops ticking.*

*Or maybe Cross stops hearing it.*

*It's hard to tell the difference.*

*Actual light.*

*It comes from nowhere.*

*It comes from everywhere.*

---

*An ocean.*
